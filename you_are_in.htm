What you just did

That wall of characters was not encrypted. It was never hidden. It was ordinary text wearing a costume called base64, and all you did was take the costume off.

Base64 exists because a lot of the internet only carries text. Email, chat, config files, URLs. If you need to move something that is not text through a pipe that only understands text, you dress it up first. Then whoever is on the other end undresses it. That is the entire idea, and there is no secret in it anywhere.

Which is worth saying plainly, because people forget it constantly and then put things in base64 believing they have hidden them.

How to spot it from across the room

Only 64 characters ever show up: A–Z, a–z, 0–9, + and /. No spaces. No punctuation. Its length always lands on a multiple of four, and when the data does not divide evenly it pads the end with = until it does.

The thing you decoded ended in ==. That was the tell, and now that you have seen it once you will not stop seeing it. Tokens, config files, things people paste into chat assuming nobody reads them. Look for the tail.

What the command actually said

echo 'aHR0cHM6Ly8...'  # hand this text to whatever comes next
        |            # the pipe. the important part.
  base64 -d          # take the costume off

That vertical bar is a pipe. It takes whatever the thing on the left produced and feeds it straight into the thing on the right, without either of them knowing about the other. Almost everything you do from here is going to be some version of that move. You just used it before you knew its name.
