# Writeups

Your folder. One writeup per mission, and it is where your portfolio starts. A
real reverse engineer keeps notes. This is you becoming one.

## The shape

When you crack a door, make a folder named for the mission and drop your evidence
in it:

```
writeups/
  mission-04/
    notes.md        <- how you got in, in your words
    solve.py        <- any code you wrote to win (optional)
```

`notes.md` is the one that matters. Three honest sentences beat three paragraphs
of filler. See **EXAMPLE-lab_strings/notes.md** for the shape. That example is
done on a practice lab on purpose, so it spoils nothing you have to earn.

## One rule

**Show the evidence, not the answer.** Paste the `strings` hit or the disassembly
line that gave it away. Do not paste the password itself. A writeup that hands
over the answer is useless to future-you, who wants to remember the *trick*, not
the string. It also keeps answers out of the repo history, where they do not
belong.

## Keep them or commit them

Keeping them on your machine is fine. Committing them is better: then they travel
with the repo and your handler reads them in your pull request. Your call, one
mission at a time.
